# Disambiguating Italian *idea* and *intuizione*

**Status:** semantic inventory and proposed roots, **NOT a lexical ratification**.
**Author decision (2026-11-14):** reject catch-all `ide` or any synonymous
catch-all. Lexical semantic specificity is ratified in
[`vocabulary_design_rules.md`](vocabulary_design_rules.md).

## 1. Existing lexical commitments (no new root)

| Source expression | Exact category | Existing root or method | Semantic caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `idea di una ruota` | abstract concept | `kon`, `rodakon` | concept is not an instance |
| `ho l'idea che P` (ordinary belief) | belief/assent | `kred` | does not entail truth |
| `ho intenzione di V` | commitment to perform an action | `int` | not necessarily performed |
| `sto pensando a X` | cognitive processing | `pens` | does not entail conclusion |
| `ho capito X` | understanding | `kap` | distinct from knowing facts `sap` |
| `so che P` | knowledge | `sap` | knowledge vs belief distinction preserved |
| `è possibile che P` | possibility | `pos` | neither prediction nor proposal |
| `P è probabile al x%` | scalar probability | `lik` + licensed quantitative frame | not the same as hunch |

These are **semantic mappings**, not blanket authorizations for invented surface
sentences or a one-to-one translation from Italian. `int`, `pens`, `kred`
and `kon` remain different.

## 2. Proposed narrow lexical gaps (not yet ratified)

All surface forms below are **candidates only**, phonotactically compatible
and checked against the current lexical index. In particular, there is no
`ide` catch-all and no generic `intu` in this proposal.

| Proposed root | Narrow denotation | Positive example | Explicit non-meanings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `propoz` | a proposal **put forward for somebody's consideration**; acceptance undecided | a suggestion submitted to colleagues | unexpressed thought, a detailed plan, an accepted commitment |
| `program` | an **organized sequence of intended actions** towards a goal | an itinerary of actions for a project | mere intention, a proposal without sequence, a prediction |
| `solv` | solving a specified problem, i.e. finding an answer satisfying the problem's constraints | obtain a valid solution | unrelated novel thought, question, arbitrary concept |
| `imagin` | form or sustain a **mental depiction** of a scene/object/event | visualize an unobserved situation | belief that depiction is real, understanding or strategy |
| `supoz` | **assumption adopted provisionally for consideration** without committing to truth | assume a cause pending checks | knowledge, justified inference, unconditional belief |
| `previs` | forecast a future state **on some articulated basis** | forecast ridership using observations | mere expectation, normative intention, mysterious premonition |
| `presag` | **anticipatory hunch about a future state**, without an articulated evidential chain | foreboding about an event | evidence-backed forecast, knowledge, completed discovery |
| `intui` | **apprehend a connection or solution directly without conscious stepwise inference** | sudden recognition of an explanation | automatically true knowledge, any ungrounded hunch, generic original idea |

The `-or`, `-it`, `-kon` and other currently productive derivations
**must not** be assumed valid for these candidates until their lexical category,
valency, and final acceptance are decided. In particular, `intui` is a
candidate **verb/process**, not a general noun 'intuition'; whether to use it,
or to express its meaning compositionally from `kap` plus an explicit manner,
remains open. A hunch about tomorrow is NOT the same as a successful immediate
grasp of a logical connection.

Etymological motivations are approximate; final sound shape and cross-language
recognizability require separate review:
- `propoz`: proposition/proposal family, a submitted proposal.
- `program`: international *programma/program* family, organized sequence.
- `solv`: Latin *solvere*, English *solve*, Romance *resolver/risolvere*.
- `imagin`: international *imagine/imaginare/imaginar* family.
- `supoz`: international *suppose/supporre/suponer* family.
- `previs`: Latin/Romance *previsione/prévision/previsão*.
- `presag`: Romance *presagio/presagiar*, foreboding.
- `intui`: Latin *intueri*, contemporary *intuire/intuition*, used in a **narrowly restricted** sense.

## 3. Mapping of troublesome Italian examples

| Italian sentence | Target interpretation | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Ho un'idea di cosa sia una ruota." | concept | `kon` / `rodakon` (existing) |
| "Ho un'idea da proporre al consiglio." | offer a proposal for review | `propoz` candidate |
| "Ho un'idea precisa di come eseguire le operazioni." | planned sequence of operations | `program` candidate |
| "Mi è venuta l'idea per risolvere questo problema." | found a candidate answer | `solv` candidate; success must be explicit |
| "Ho in mente l'immagine di un ponte." | mental depiction | `imagin` candidate |
| "La mia idea è che sia colpevole." | belief or provisional assumption depending on commitment | `kred` existing / `supoz` candidate |
| "Ho un'idea di come andrà il mercato." | justified future prediction or hunch depending on evidence | `previs` / `presag` candidates |
| "Ho intuito come funzionava il problema." | non-discursive recognition of a connection | `intui` candidate, not a guarantee of truth |
| "Ho l'intuizione che domani pioverà." | hunch / foreboding | `presag` candidate, **not** `intui` |
| "Ho avuto un'intuizione geniale per il progetto." | sudden recognition or candidate solution, depending on what happened | `intui` or `solv`, not a generic name |
| "Mi sono fatto un'idea dell'aspetto dell'edificio." | mental depiction or general understanding depending on context | `imagin` candidate or `kap` existing |

## 4. Decisions required before approving candidates

1. Is `propoz` restricted to externally submitted suggestions, or may a
   speaker describe an *uncommunicated* candidate proposal? Avoid two senses.
2. Should `program` name the full sequence, or a verb for devising it?
   Do not derive a noun arbitrarily from the verb without agreement.
3. Should `solv` assert **successful** solution, or merely a proposed
   answer? These entailments must be separate.
4. Is a mental depiction `imagin` distinct from a model / simulation?
5. Should `supoz` commit to an assumption in reasoning or merely entertaining
   a possibility? Distinguish from `kred`, `pos`, `lik`.
6. How strong must evidence be before `previs` is licensed, and does
   `presag` imply an emotional foreboding rather than neutral weak expectation?
7. Can non-discursive recognition be expressed compositionally using
   `kap` plus a manner modifier, obviating `intui` entirely?

Do **not** ratify these eight candidates simply because they are listed. The
choice is guided by contrastive examples and realistic usage, not by a desire
to translate the surface word *idea*.
