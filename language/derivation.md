# Derivational Morphology

Derivational morphology builds lexical items before case marking. Nominal
plurality with `wi` is analytic and therefore precedes the resulting noun
phrase rather than attaching inside the derived word.

## 1. Productive suffixes

| Function | Suffix | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| person / agent | `-or` | `fakor` | maker / doer |
| tool / instrument / device | `-il` | `fakil` | making tool/device |
| place / institution / field / domain | `-ia` | `fakia` | workshop / making domain |
| participial / resultative | `-it` | `fakit` | done / made |
| abstract quality / state / property | `-itat` | `klaritat` | clarity |
| relational adjective | `-al` | `fakal` | related to making |
| passive possibility | `-abil` | `movabil` | capable of being moved |

The independent approved **numeric scalar-factor suffix `-fol`** is
defined in [numerals](numerals.md), not as a general lexical agent or
instrument suffix. It applies **only to positive canonical cardinals**:
`bisfol` = double, `terfol` = triple, `forfol` = quadruple. Do not
confuse it with the separate `NUMjer` iteration operator. The earlier
candidate `-mul` was **not ratified**.

### Passive possibility `-abil` (author ratification 2026-11-14)

The productive suffix **`-abil`** creates an adjective from a canonical
non-modal verbal root whose event can affect a patient: *capable of undergoing
that action* under relevant circumstances. It does **not** assert that the
action occurred or that someone intended or designed the patient for it.
Examples: `movabil` (movable), `vidabil` (visible), `dikabil` (sayable).
This is a passive potential interpretation; `-abil` is **not** an independent
lexical root and does not mean 'I enable' or 'to enable'. Existing `pot`
expresses an actor's capacity, not this passive property.

A general **designed-for** derivation remains unratified. `-al` is relational,
`-il` is an instrument or device, and `-it` is resultative, not potential.
The canonical state-phase family `es / ines / ades / dees / rees` concerns
being, remaining, entering, leaving and returning to a state. None of these
forms inherently means *able to be X* or *made capable of X*. The provisional
root `apt` is not authorized as an alternative derivational suffix.

## 2. Agent and instrumental derivation

The productive suffix **`-or`** derives a person whose characteristic role or
activity is to perform the action expressed by the base. It is the canonical
person/agent suffix; historical `-er` is retired from this function.

The productive suffix **`-il`** derives an object, tool, machine, device, or
artifact whose characteristic function is to perform, facilitate, or enable
the action expressed by the base.

Examples:

- `fak` → `fakor` — maker / person who makes
- `fak` → `fakil` — making tool / device
- `dok` → `dokor` — teacher / person who teaches
- `vid` → `vidil` — viewing / observation instrument

Derivational parsing is based on a canonical base plus a productive suffix,
not on spelling alone. A lexical or grammatical word that merely ends in
`-or` is not automatically segmented. In particular, `major` and `minor` are
indivisible comparison words, not agentive derivations.

The free relator `per` remains the analytic instrumental/perlative marker
("through; by means of"). Thus `-il` creates the lexical name of an instrument,
while `per + NP` marks use of a noun phrase as the means in a clause.

## 3. `-ia` is hiatus

`-ia` remains canonical and is pronounced **/i.a/**. Adjacent written vowels
remain vowels; the suffix is not `/ja/`.

Examples:

- `fak + ia` → `fakia` /fa.ki.a/
- `dok + ia` → `dokia` /do.ki.a/
- `med + ia` → `media` /me.di.a/

## 4. Participial/resultative `-it`

`-it` is productive on verb roots:

- `dik` → `dikit` — said
- `mov` → `movit` — moved
- `vid` → `vidit` — seen
- `fak` → `fakit` — done / made

The same `-it` form may be zero-nominalized as a result noun. Thus `fakit` can
mean a fact / thing done when used nominally; `wi fakit` is the explicit plural
and `bis fakit` means two facts / two things done. Nominalized participles take
ordinary noun number and case marking and do not receive a special plural
suffix.

## 5. Abstract `-itat`

`-itat` replaces the historical abstract use of `-it`:

- `bon` → `bonitat` — goodness
- `klar` → `klaritat` — clarity
- `uman` → `umanitat` — humanity

## 6. Other productive suffixes

- `-or` — person/agent
- `-il` — tool/instrument/device
- `-al` — relational adjective
- `-abil` — passive possibility adjective

## 7. Prefixes retained from the original system

The following productive prefixes remain available unless a future lexical
collision requires a root-specific revision:

`ne-`, `anti-`, `de-`, `re-`, `pre-`, `pos-`, `kun-`, `auto-`, `semi-`,
`multi-`, `neo-`, `super-`, `sub-`, `mikro-`, `makro-`.

Examples must use current roots, e.g. `refak`, `defak`, `autofak`, not obsolete
`refac`, `defac`, `autofac`.

### Antonymic `ne-` versus free negation `no`

**Ratified 2026-10-24.** Productive `ne-` forms the *semantic opposite* of a
property with an identifiable opposite pole, not simple logical negation.
It is written fused to the lexical root. By contrast, independent `no`
negates the truth of a predicate. The historical derivational prefix `no-`
is **retired**; it must not be used to build new lexemes. Active opposition
`anti-` remains distinct from both.

| Construction | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `tik bel` | this is beautiful |
| `tik no bel` | this is not beautiful; neutral or ugly remain possible |
| `tik nebel` | this is ugly; aesthetic negative pole |
| `tik no nebel` | this is not ugly; neutral or beautiful remain possible |
| `bon` / `nebon` | generally good / bad |
| `prob` / `neprob` | morally good / morally bad |
| `just` / `nejust` | just / unjust, in a substantive sense |
| `apt` / `neapt` | fit / unfit for purpose (illustrative; `apt` awaits separate lexicon ratification) |

Not all categories have scalar antonyms: `nedom` is not a licensed example
and a nominal `dom` does not automatically acquire an antonym. In particular,
`nemoral` (if used) must be semantically resolved as an actual contrary to
morality, not mere absence of moral relevance. The semantics of `ne-` are
constrained by lexical opposability, not by arbitrary string prefixing.




### Normative antonyms: four distinct domains (ratified 2026-10-24)

| Domain | Positive | Contrary | Meaning of contrary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moral character | `prob` | `neprob` | morally bad |
| Justice/fairness | `just` | `nejust` | unjust |
| Applicable positive law | `legal` | `nelegal` | illegal/unlawful |
| Sacred or religious order | `fas` | `nefas` | impious/sacrilegious |

The independent particle `no` negates the stated predicate without asserting
its contrary: `no legal` does not necessarily mean `nelegal`; `no fas`
does not necessarily mean `nefas`. In particular, `nejust` means unjust,
not illegal. A binary contextual distinction may yield equivalent extensions,
but the expressions remain semantically different. `ne-` is licensed on
opposable qualitative bases, not arbitrary nouns or category labels.

### Canonical phase prefixes

A restricted, semantically compositional phase family is canonical with the
prefixes `in-`, `ad-`, `de-`, and `re-`. The prefix and root are written as one
word, without a hyphen. Their invariant phase meanings are:

- `in-` — remain / continue in the base event or state
- `ad-` — enter / begin the base event or state
- `de-` — leave / cease the base event or state
- `re-` — return / resume the base event or state

With `es`, this yields `ines`, `ades`, `dees`, `rees`. With `mov`, the same
architecture yields `inmov`, `admov`, `demov`, `remov`: continue moving, begin
moving, cease moving, and resume moving. The exact lexical translation depends
on the semantic type of the base, but the phase relation contributed by the
prefix remains constant.

These phase prefixes do not encode progressive/perfect aspect. Finite aspect
remains the separate canonical `Ø/e/o` slot.

### `mov` valency alternation

The `mov` family has a canonical intransitive/transitive valency alternation.
With no accusative object, the subject is the moving entity; with an accusative
`X-em`, the subject is the external mover/causer and `X-em` is the entity whose
motion is controlled.

```text
X-NOM mov                 = X moves
Y-NOM mov X-em            = Y moves / displaces X
Y-NOM mov X-em DEST-ad    = Y moves / displaces X toward DEST
```

The same alternation applies compositionally to the phase-derived members:

- `X admov` = X begins moving; `Y admov X-em` = Y sets X in motion
- `X inmov` = X continues moving; `Y inmov X-em` = Y keeps X in motion
- `X demov` = X stops moving; `Y demov X-em` = Y stops X
- `X remov` = X resumes moving; `Y remov X-em` = Y sets X moving again / restarts X's motion

`inmov` does **not** mean "push" or "pull". It contributes only continuation
of motion; the physical manner or force relation of pushing and pulling is a
separate lexical matter. Likewise, this valency alternation does not replace
finite aspect morphology: progressive `mov-e-...` means that motion is ongoing,
whereas `inmov` presupposes an already established motion and asserts its
continuation.


### Vertical motion: `alt`, `bas`, `altmov`, `basmov`

The canonical vertical spatial axis uses `alt` = high / upper / up and `bas` =
low / lower / down. Direction can be expressed analytically with the ordinary
allative case:

```text
mov altad    = move / go upward
mov basad    = move / go downward
```

The transparent compounds `altmov` and `basmov` are also canonical:

- `altmov` = ascend / go up
- `basmov` = descend / go down

These are directional compounds, not phase forms. In particular, `admov` still
means "begin moving" and `demov` still means "cease moving". The ordinary
`mov`-family valency alternation can apply where semantically appropriate; the
vertical element contributes direction, while `mov` contributes motion.


### Explicit transition between states

The canonical verb `traes` expresses transition from one explicitly named state
to another. Its frame reuses the synthetic case system:

```text
traes DEST-ad SOURCE-de
```

Thus `traes solidad likwidde` means "pass from liquid to solid". This differs
from `ades solid`, which simply means "become solid" and leaves the source state
unspecified. `traes` does not create a new phase prefix and does not replace
`ades`; it is the two-pole transition construction.

A second canonical `traes` frame is transitive and causative:

```text
Y-NOM traes X-em RESULT-da
```

Here `-em` marks the entity being transformed and resultative/transformative
`-da` marks the state, role, class, or property produced by the transformation.
For example, `Y traes akwaem solidda` means "Y transforms the water, making it
solid". `-da` is a nominal case relation in this frame, not a derivational
suffix and not a replacement for allative `-ad`.

## 8. Inflection after derivation

Case suffixes follow the fully derived lexical word:

- `fakorem` — maker as direct object
- `klaritatem` — clarity as direct object
- `fakias` — of the workshop/domain

Explicit plural `wi` remains a separate pre-nominal particle:

- `wi fakor` — makers
- `wi fakorem` — makers as direct object

The historical nominal plural suffix `-en` is not part of the current system.

### Ratified medium-quality lexical compound (2026-10-24)

- `medi` is the descriptive middle/intermediate position on a scale.
- `medibon` is the ratified lexical compound `medi` + `bon`: average-quality,
  ordinary, unexceptional. It does not lexically require a negative judgment;
  context can convey the evaluative sense of "mediocre".
- `subbon` expresses falling short of the relevant standard of quality, not
  mere statistical averageness. This is a compositional illustration, not a
  new standalone lexicon entry.
- `submedi` (below a mean) and `supermedi` (well above a mean) are compositional
  illustrations, not independently ratified lexical entries. `nemedi` is not
  automatically licensed because "middle" has no unique opposite pole.

The lexical ratification of `medibon` does **not** establish a general
productive two-root compounding rule. Such a rule requires a separate review
of the language's compounding syntax and collision constraints.

### Absolute degree versus relative superlative (ratified 2026-10-24)

Relative extrema use the free grammatical operators `masim` (most)
and `minim` (least), not an affix. Adjectival `super-` is not a
productive absolute-superlative construction: do not equate `superbel`
with "very beautiful" or "most beautiful" on this basis. Other
historical derivational meanings of `super-` remain unaffected.
Absolute intensity is a separate analytic domain with ratified free
modifiers `minu`, `pok`, `mult`, `ekstrem` (see `comparison.md`).
These are not derivational affixes or relative superlatives.

### Cognitive and physical-action root families (ratified revision 2026-10-25)

The confirmed root distinctions are: `pens` = think / mentally process;
`kap` = understand / cognitively grasp meaning; `sap` = know / possess
knowledge; `fer` = physically take / grasp; `portar` = carry / transport.
The former senses `kap` = take/capture and `sap` = understand are retired.
The etymology of `fer` (Latin *ferre*, 'carry') must not override its explicitly
ratified Unisermo meaning 'take/grasp'; `portar` denotes carrying/transport.

Each verb forms its **own regular phase family** using the already canonical
prefixes, with no borrowed idiomatic senses:

| Base | Begin (`ad-`) | Continue (`in-`) | Cease (`de-`) | Resume (`re-`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fer` | `adfer` | `infer` | `defer` | `refer` |
| `portar` | `adportar` | `inportar` | `deportar` | `reportar` |
| `kap` | `adkap` | `inkap` | `dekap` | `rekap` |
| `sap` | `adsap` | `insap` | `desap` | `resap` |
| `pens` | `adpens` | `inpens` | `depens` | `repens` |

For example, `deportar` means cease carrying, not deportar in the ordinary
Italian/English sense; `reportar` means resume carrying, not reportar.
Phase readings of stative cognition (`kap`, `sap`) are subject to the
ordinary meaning of a state; they are not a new lexical sense.

### Associative `kun-` and the `ten` family (ratified 2026-10-25)

`ten` means to hold/maintain a physical or control **constraint over time**,
not to preserve a property or state as such. State persistence is already
expressed by `ines` from `es`. `fer` is physical acquisition and
`portar` carrying/transport. The ordinary phase family is `adten` begin
holding, `inten` continue holding, `deten` cease holding/release the
constraint (without entailing motion), and `reten` resume holding.

The **productive bound prefix `kun-`** contributes jointness/association to
the base event, without itself entailing enclosure, collaboration, or a new
participant. Illustrative compositional forms include `kunmov` move
jointly, `kunportar` carry jointly, `kunpens` think jointly, and `kunfak`
do/make jointly. Invariant **free** `kun` remains a comitative relator
introducing an associated participant: the prefix modifies the event,
whereas the relator introduces a noun phrase. These examples do not ratify
unrestricted two-root compounds.

`kunten` is additionally **lexically specialized** to physical/spatial
containment: maintain contents within spatial bounds. This is a documented
special meaning, not an automatic interpretation of `kun-` across other
roots. Abstract/informational part-whole inclusion continues to use `part`
and the ordinary genitive rather than this spatial sense.

**Ratified 2026-10-25:** the historical associative prefix `ko-` is retired
in favor of the sole canonical productive associative prefix `kun-`.
The earlier `ko-` already meant together/joint/co-, as documented in the
immutable legacy derivation specification. Historical forms like `kofac`
remain historical only; modern forms use the current root and prefix,
e.g. `kunfak`. This is distinct from the already retired nominal
comitative suffix `-ko`; free `kun` remains the canonical comitative relator.

### Physical opening and closing properties (ratified 2026-10-25)

`aper` (physically open configuration) and `klus` (physically closed
configuration; Latin *clusus/clausus*) are independent property roots.
Neither means 'begin' or 'end', and neither automatically applies to
figurative opening/closing of a process. They are not simply exhaustive
logical negations. Model B expresses state entry with `ades aper` or
`ades klus`, persistence with `ines`, and physical causation with the
already canonical `Y traes X-em aper-da` / `Y traes X-em klus-da`.

The verb root `portar` replaces former `port` (carry/transport).
`porta` independently denotes a physical door. The regular third-person
singular of `portar` is `portara`, avoiding the former collision where
`port` + third-person `-a` yielded `porta`. Phase derivatives are
`adportar`, `inportar`, `deportar`, `reportar`, and productive
`kunportar` means transport jointly; none inherit unrelated Romance
lexical meanings.

## Numeric ordinal marker `-im` (author ratification 24 October 2026)

The ordinal `NUMim` is an invariant, productive **numeral modifier**,
not a general-purpose derivational suffix on arbitrary roots. It
attaches to an entire positive cardinal. When the cardinal ends in
`o`, only that final vowel contracts (`uno` -> `unim`,
`oto` -> `otim`, `dekuno` -> `dekunim`).
Accepted noncanonical analytic spellings (`unoim` / `otoim`)
normalize to the contracted canonical form. `nulim` is not
ratified. See [numerals](numerals.md) section 8.

### Canonical physical-action argument frames (ratified 2026-11-13)

The distinct meanings of `fer`, `portar`, `ten` and the
**lexicalized** compound `kunten` also govern distinct thematic roles.
All four select an accusative `X-em` participant in the canonical
**overt-argument** construction. No case suffix, morph or lexical root
is introduced.

| Head | Canonical frame | Semantic roles |
|---|---|---|
| `fer` | `Y-NOM fer X-em [SOURCE-de]` | Y **acquirer/taker**, X physically grasped/acquired; optional SOURCE physical origin |
| `portar` | `Y-NOM portar X-em [SOURCE-de] [DEST-ad]` | Y **carrier**, X transported cargo; optional spatial origin and destination |
| `ten` | `Y-NOM ten X-em` | Y holder/controller, X the entity constrained or held over time |
| `kunten` | `CONTAINER-NOM kunten CONTENTS-em` | subject physical **container**, object physical contents |

These are **different semantic relations with the same syntactic
accusative**. `fer` does not imply transport, ownership transfer
or permission. `portar` entails a transporting/carrying event, but
`DEST-ad` marks direction **toward** a destination, never proof
of arrival. `ten` specifies maintained physical/control constraint
without acquisition or displacement. `kunten` specifies spatial
containment; its subject need not be animate, conscious or volitional.
Neither `ten` nor `kunten` acquires an inherent motion SOURCE-de
or DEST-ad argument.

The optional `SOURCE-de` with `fer` names a **physical
origin/source**, not necessarily a previous owner or permission
grantor. With `portar`, optional SOURCE-de and DEST-ad
describe the spatial transport event, not ownership transfer.
Ordinary free relators and an event-location `LOCATION-in`
remain independent adjuncts, not additional lexical participants.
A locative `-in` does not become a second contents argument
of `kunten`.

Examples with existing attested roots:

- `i feri portaem` — I physically take/grasp the door.
- `i feri portaem umande` — I take the door from a person's
  physical location, with no ownership assertion.
- `i portari portaem domad` — I carry the door toward a house.
- `i portari portaem domde umanad` — I carry the door
  from a house toward a person.
- `i teni portaem` — I hold the door / maintain control of it.
- `dom kuntena akwaem` — the house physically contains water.

A **single** regular phase prefix `ad-/in-/de-/re-` on
these verbs preserves the participant roles of its base frame
while changing the phase of that event; the already ratified
productive associative `kun-` likewise preserves those roles
and adds association/jointness without introducing a mandatory
new co-agent. The separately **lexicalized** `kunten` keeps its
containment meaning: it is not decoded as a generic
`kun-` + `ten` event. Applying a further associative
`kun-` to the lexicalized `kunten` (`kunkunten`) is
outside this bounded single-layer parser subset.
No general stacked prefix derivation is approved by this rule.

**Important implementation limit:** one explicit accusative
participant is **required in the currently approved parser
subset** for these four frames. Whether Unisermo licenses
contextual or discourse-conditioned omission of this participant
is a **separate unresolved grammatical question**. Do not
infer that it is universally forbidden in the language.

See [verb_specification.md](verb_specification.md),
[LDR-0014](../docs/ldr/LDR-0014-physical-action-valency.md) and
[formalization/data/valency_frames.json](../formalization/data/valency_frames.json).


## Vehicle vocabulary and bounded noun compounds (author ratification 2026-11-14)

The vehicle system favors short independent internationally recognizable nouns:

| Word | Canonical meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `kar` | car, automobile |
| `bus` | bus |
| `tren` | train (unchanged earlier ratification) |
| `moto` | motorcycle |
| `kam` | truck/lorry |
| `van` | light cargo van |
| `bik` | bicycle |
| `karg` | cargo/freight, **not** truck |
| `ped` | foot, **not** bicycle |
| `via` | physical way/path for travel, not an abstract itinerary |

The **bound** diminutive prefix `mini-` indicates a vehicle relatively smaller or with reduced capacity **within its base category**. No numeric size threshold or legal classification is implied. Ratified lexical results are `minibus`, `minikar`, `minivan`, and `minitren`. It is not a second degree word equivalent to `minu`, and it is not the SI prefix `mikro`. No stacking of `mini-` and no unrestricted application to unrelated nouns follows from this ratification. A hyphen is part of *lexicon notation* for the prefix, not surface spelling of its approved compounds.

Three explicitly licensed noun compounds are `karvia` (road suitable for vehicles), `trenvia` (railway infrastructure) and `bikvia` (cycle path). They follow **vehicle noun + via**, with physical transit infrastructure as the semantic head. These ratified examples do **not** license arbitrary two-noun concatenation or identify a railway as an operating service. `tren` remains the basic noun for train; no `trenvek` or mandatory `-kar` vehicle suffix is adopted. `vek` is not ratified.

The ratified compounds are entered as canonical whole nouns and take nominal case on the **whole word** (`minibusem`, `trenviaem`, `bikviaad`). The current deterministic parser recognizes these licensed nouns; a general compound grammar is intentionally deferred.


## Wheel noun (author ratification 2026-11-14)

`roda` is the canonical common noun for **wheel** (Italian *ruota*): a generally circular mechanical component turning around an axle, used for rolling, support or motion transmission. The choice favors recognizability across Romance forms (Portuguese/Catalan/Galician *roda* and Latin *rota*) and replaces the unratified candidates `rot` and `rota`. It is an independent noun and takes ordinary nominal cases: `roda`, `rodaem`, `rodas`, `rodaad`, `rodade`, `rodain`, `rodada`. `roda` does not itself assert motion or specify that the wheel belongs to a vehicle. No productive new compounds or wheel-count vehicle subclassifications are ratified by this decision.
