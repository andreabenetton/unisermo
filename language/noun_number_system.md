# Noun Number System

## 1. Number-neutral nouns

A bare noun is grammatically neutral with respect to number. Singular or plural
reference may be recovered from context, from verbal number, or from an overt
numeral/quantifier.

Examples:

- `uman` — person / people, number unspecified
- `dom` — house / houses, number unspecified

## 2. Optional explicit plural particle `wi`

When explicit nominal plurality is useful, place the invariant particle `wi`
before the noun or noun phrase.

- `wi uman` — people, explicitly plural
- `wi umano` — men
- `wi umane` — women

Canonical pronunciation is `/wi/`; `[u̯i]` is an accepted accent-tolerant
realization.

`wi` marks plurality only. It does not mark definiteness.

## 3. Numerals and quantifiers

When a numeral or a quantity-encoding quantifier already expresses number,
`wi` is normally omitted:

- `uno dom` — one house / a house
- `ter dom` — three houses
- `multi dom` — many houses
- `nul dom` — no house
- `omni uman` — all/every person

## 4. Verb number without noun marking

Subject plurality may be expressed by the finite verb while the noun remains
bare:

- `dom mova` — a/the house moves
- `dom movan` — houses move


## 5. Nominalized participles

A participial/resultative form in `-it` may be used directly as a noun. When it
is nominal, it follows exactly the same number rules as any other noun:

- `fakit` — fact / facts, number unspecified
- `wi fakit` — facts, explicitly plural
- `bis fakit` — two facts; no `wi`
- `nul fakit` — no facts; no `wi`

There is no special participial plural ending.

## 6. Removed legacy suffix `-en`

The former optional nominal plural suffix `-en` is obsolete. It is not part of
the current noun-number system and must not be added after numerals or case
suffixes.
