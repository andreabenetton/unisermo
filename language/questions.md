# Questions

## 1. Inventory

| Form | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `ka` | polar | yes/no question marker |
| `kid` | wh | what |
| `kis` | wh | who |
| `kos` | wh | where |
| `kud` | wh | from where |
| `kan` | wh | when |
| `kom` | wh | how |
| `ker` | wh | why |
| `kot` | wh | how many |
| `kal` | wh | what kind |

`ker` replaces the historical `kur`, freeing `kurar` for care/cure.

## 2. Polar questions

Place `ka` at clause start; ordinary clause morphology remains unchanged:

- `ka u vidu?` — do you see?
- `ka u vidu domem?` — do you see the house?
- `ka wi amik movan?` — do the friends move?

Negation stays immediately before the verb:

- `ka u no vidu?` — don't you see?

## 3. Content questions

Pronominal `kid` and `kis` may take case suffixes. Adverbial `kos`, `kud`,
`kan`, `kom`, `ker` remain invariant. Modifier `kot` and `kal` precede a noun.

Examples:

- `kis vida?` — who sees?
- `kidem vidu?` — what do you see?
- `kidad movan?` — toward what do they move?
- `kos movan?` — where do they move?
- `kud movan?` — from where do they move?
- `kan movdi?` — when did I move?
- `kom faki?` — how do I make/do it?
- `ker fakdi?` — why did I do/make it?
- `kot dom vidu?` — how many houses do you see?
- `kal dom vidu?` — what kind of house do you see?

After `kot`, the noun normally remains bare because quantity is already
expressed; optional `wi` is unnecessary.
