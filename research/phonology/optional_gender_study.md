# Optional referential-specificity marker study

> **Status:** confirmed design decision. This report reconstructs the completed post-`011247a` study from the persisted quantitative findings and the project-owner confirmation supplied when resuming the repository. The formerly open ordering question is now resolved: plural `/N/` may be medial before optional `-o/-e`, so `ano/ane` are canonical.

## Decision

GlottoDelta remains grammatically gender-neutral by default.

- neutral / unspecified reference = zero marking
- optional masculine or male-specific reference = `-o`
- optional feminine or female-specific reference = `-e`

These are **semantic/referential specificity markers**, not grammatical gender classes. They never trigger obligatory agreement on verbs, adjectives, articles, or other words. The neutral form always remains valid.

For third person:

| Reference | Singular | Plural |
|---|---:|---:|
| neutral / unspecified | `a` | `an` |
| masculine / male-specific | `ao` | `ano` |
| feminine / female-specific | `ae` | `ane` |

The confirmed morphological order is:

`person + plural N + optional specificity marker`

The previously confirmed plural morphophoneme `/N/` therefore remains final in neutral forms such as `in`, `un`, and `an`, but may be medial specifically when an optional specificity suffix follows it, as in `ano` and `ane`.

## Inventory and accent results

The completed study compared strict mid-vowel marking with an accent-tolerant front/back system and retained the latter.

| system | demographic P | source-language L |
|---|---:|---:|
| strict simultaneous `/e/ + /o/` | ≈ 77.44% | ≈ 72.95% |
| accent-tolerant front/back (`e~i`, `o~u`) | ≈ 99.51% | ≈ 97.97% |
| existing `a/i/u + N` subsystem | ≈ 98.67% | ≈ 88.97% |

Accent tolerance is asymmetric only in phonetic realization, not in function:

- canonical feminine `-e` may be realized toward `[i]` in accents without stable mid vowels;
- canonical masculine `-o` may be realized toward `[u]`;
- the front/back distinction must remain recoverable;
- when specificity is intended, the two markers may never collapse into the same realization;
- where the distinction cannot be maintained reliably, the neutral form or a lexical paraphrase remains available.

## Seed morphology pressure test

The study applied both markers to the existing 74-root seed:

- 74 roots × 2 markers = **148 generated forms**
- marker-induced consonant clusters: **0 / 148**
- vowel-hiatus boundaries: **8 / 148 = 5.41%**
- collisions with existing neutral seed roots: **0**
- masculine/feminine generated collisions: **0**

This favored vowel suffixes over consonantal alternatives: they introduce no new consonant clusters in the tested seed and preserve a transparent optional layer after the person/number morphology.

## Resolution of the previously open question

Before confirmation, the phonotactically preferred proposal was `a/an -> ao/ae, ano/ane`, but the study left one architectural question open: whether plural `/N/` was allowed to cease being word-final when followed by optional specificity marking.

That question is now explicitly resolved **yes**. `/N/` is the plural morphophoneme after the person vowel, not a requirement that the plural nasal occupy absolute word-final position. Its normal neutral forms remain `in/un/an`; `ano/ane` are the confirmed third-person plural specific forms.

## Consequence for subsequent work

Family/genealogical dispersion research must treat this specificity system as already confirmed. It is not an open candidate comparison, and the genealogy inventory task does not reopen it.
