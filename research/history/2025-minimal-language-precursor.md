The dates below refer to retrieved speaker-attributed *conversation-index summaries*, not an exported full transcript. Quoted concepts are paraphrases unless independently copied from original text.

### Consequential distinction

The evidence supports an **earlier interest in communication efficiency and an explicitly constructed language**. It does **not** establish a name, repository, longitudinal continuation, or any artifact proving that the July 2025 proposal was implemented. Nor does it establish that *the 2026 project* began in July 2025.

It is therefore misleading to promote the July 2025 sketch to “Unisermo v0”, or to interpret its suggestions as settled user choices. A separate **16 May 2026** original user message is the earliest recovered *explicit international-language vowel design* in the presently documentable 2026 construction sequence (see [early May evidence](early-may-conversation-evidence.md)).

### Methodological implications for the future paper

1. The **earliest observed conception of a *minimal artificial language*** in the conversation record is July 2025; the **earliest directly recoverable *systematic international-language phonology*** starts May 2026. These are **different historical claims**.
2. A historical paper could make the limited claim that the author had **previously contemplated a minimal artificial language**, but should not say that the 2025 sketch is an ancestor/version of Unisermo without an intervening link.
3. If the full July 2025 thread or messages between July 2025 and May 2026 are recovered, investigate whether the criterion “same information in less speaking time” recurs explicitly in 2026. Resemblance of goals does not prove development continuity.
4. The 2025 sketch does **not** supply measurements of semantic efficiency, learning effort, information rate or intelligibility. Its values are exploratory assumptions.
5. The first preserved 2026 specifications clearly depart from extreme lexical minimalism toward **selective phonological richness plus conservative high-frequency vocabulary**. This is a meaningful **comparison of documented design strategies**, not necessarily an actual amendment of one pre-existing specification.
