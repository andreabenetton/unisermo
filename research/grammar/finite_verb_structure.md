# Finite Verb Structure

Status: **confirmed**.

## Canonical morphotactics

Every ordinary finite verb has the fixed order:

```text
root + aspect + time + person vowel + optional N
```

Person vowels are:

- `i` = first person
- `u` = second person
- `a` = third person

The plural morphophoneme `/N/` follows the person vowel when the subject is plural, producing `in`, `un`, `an` in ordinary orthography.

## Aspect

- `Ø` = simple
- `e` = ongoing/progressive
- `o` = completed/perfect

## Time

- `Ø` = present
- `d` = past
- `f` = future

Aspect precedes time, giving a closed 3×3 grid: `Ø/e/o`, `d/ed/od`, `f/ef/of` when read by time rows.

Examples with `dik` in first-person singular: `diki`, `dikei`, `dikoi`, `dikdi`, `dikedi`, `dikodi`, `dikfi`, `dikefi`, `dikofi`.

The aspect markers are vowels and the time markers are consonants. This division drastically reduces morphology-created consonant clusters. In present aspect-marked forms, where time is zero, aspect and person vowels are adjacent and form canonical hiatus: `dikei` = `/dik.e.i/`, `dikou` = `/dik.o.u/`. The aspect vowel must remain perceptually recoverable and may not coalesce with the person vowel if that would erase the aspect contrast.

The former finite aspect markers `n` (progressive) and `b` (completed/perfect) are retired. They remain available as ordinary lexical phonemes but no longer occupy the finite aspect slot.

## Servile/modal verbs

The former synthetic `m` ability/potential marker is retired. Meanings such as ability, obligation, volition and learned capacity are expressed analytically by an ordinary finite servile verb followed by the bare infinitive/root of the lexical verb. The servile carries aspect, time, person and number; the dependent infinitive is uninflected. Lexical roots for the individual servile verbs are decided separately.

The ordering rule is morphological, not merely orthographic: aspect never follows time, TAM never follows the person marker, and `/N/` never precedes the person vowel.
