# Finite Verb Structure

Status: **confirmed**.

## Canonical morphotactics

Every ordinary finite verb has the fixed order:

```text
root + time + aspect + person vowel + optional N
```

Person vowels are:

- `i` = first person
- `u` = second person
- `a` = third person

The plural morphophoneme `/N/` follows the person vowel when the subject is plural, producing `in`, `un`, `an` in ordinary orthography.

## Time

- `Ø` = present
- `d` = past
- `f` = future

## Aspect

- `Ø` = simple
- `n` = ongoing/progressive
- `b` = completed/perfect

Time precedes aspect, giving a closed 3×3 grid: `Ø/n/b`, `d/dn/db`, `f/fn/fb`.

Examples with `dik` in first-person singular: `diki`, `dikni`, `dikbi`, `dikdi`, `dikdni`, `dikdbi`, `dikfi`, `dikfni`, `dikfbi`.

## Servile/modal verbs

The former synthetic `m` ability/potential marker is retired. Meanings such as ability, obligation, volition and learned capacity are expressed analytically by an ordinary finite servile verb followed by the bare infinitive/root of the lexical verb. The servile carries time, aspect, person and number; the dependent infinitive is uninflected. Lexical roots for the individual servile verbs are decided separately.

The ordering rule is morphological, not merely orthographic: time never follows aspect, TAM never follows the person marker, and `/N/` never precedes the person vowel.
