# Audit of potential additional serviles — 2026-11-13

**Status:** analysis only. The author ratified **tent** as a servile. No other root is approved here.

## Current semantic inventory

| Head | Core dimension | Truth-conditional commitment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `vol` | desire / volition | no attempted action or result |
| `tent` | goal-directed attempt | attempt occurs, result unspecified |
| `pot` | participant ability | no performed action |
| `pos` | circumstantial opportunity | no performed action |
| `deb` | normative duty | no performed action |
| `aut` | permission / authority | no performed action |
| `sap` | procedural know-how, also content state | no performed action |
| `adsap` etc. | phases of procedural knowledge | no performance of dependent action |

All are expressed through the existing finite-head plus uninflected dependent-verb frame. The phase family of `sap` does not suddenly create new epistemic probability serviles.

## Candidate gap analysis, ordered by importance

### 1. Epistemic modality: probable / possible / certain that P

*Claim:* 'It may be raining', 'it must have rained, given the wet ground', and 'it probably rained' are not synonymous with `pos V` ('I have the opportunity to V') or `deb V` (normative duty).

**Likely genuine gap.** But these operators apply to a *proposition with its own subject*, not automatically to a same-subject bare verb. They may need a clause-scoped epistemic construction, not a new ordinary same-subject servile. The distinction between factual `due` and evidential `nam` is important: evidence for a claim is not the event's physical cause. Consider three values of speaker commitment (possibility, likelihood, certainty) and evidence source without copying the English ambiguous 'must/can'.

### 2. Physical necessity / inevitability

'To survive, the machine must cool' / 'This event is inevitable under these constraints' is not automatically deontic `deb` (duty) and not merely lack of an alternative action. Potentially expressible as conditional causal/general necessity with `se`/`due`, or a proposition-level necessity operator. **Open**, no new root until minimal contrasts show necessity is irreducible.

### 3. Intention vs mere desire

`vol V` currently covers wanting / intending in the lexical description. The semantic difference is real: 'I want to travel but do not intend to' is coherent. `tent V` is a *committed effort*, but not every future intention implies an attempt has already started. **Potential gap** for *plan/intend* without current effort. First attempt `vol V` plus scheduled commitment or explicit plan-construction; assess before a new servile root.

### 4. Willingness / readiness / consent

Willingness ('I am willing to help') is not exactly `vol` desire, `aut` permission, `pot` ability, or `tent` attempt. This may require dispositional adjective / ordinary predicate, or a volition complement plus condition. **Lower priority**, likely not an obligatory new servile.

### 5. Habituality / frequency / repetition

Habitually doing V and trying V are unrelated. **Do not add as servile by default**: evaluate productive TAM/aspect, repeated events, temporal quantifiers, and frequency adverbs. Habitual aspect is not automatically synonymous with progressive.

### 6. Success / completion, failure, avoidance

`tent V` makes no success claim. Successful realization can be asserted as a separate event; `no V` alone does not assert an attempt. Avoidance can be an attempt `tent no V` but successful prevention requires that V did not happen. **Do not add 'succeed' or 'fail' as servile automatically**: first test whether coordinated/scope-marked clauses or a resultative predicate can express the contrast without a new head.

### 7. Causation and permission to others

'I make someone act' / 'I permit someone to act' have **different subjects** for causer and embedded agent. Current serviles intentionally share a subject and `aut` describes permission *received* by the subject. These are **not solved by adding another ordinary same-subject servile**. Study explicit causative clauses and case arguments separately; retain the bounded `traes` resultative.

### 8. Perceptual or reportative ('seem to', 'be said to')

Evidential `nam`, inference `ergo`, and sensory roots do not alone cover all 'apparently P' readings. This is a **proposition-scope / evidentiality issue** rather than an obvious bare-verb servile; first design speaker vs observer commitment and quotation/source.

## Recommendation

**No second servile ratification now.** First examine (1) epistemic probability/necessity at the proposition level and (2) deliberate intention versus mere wanting; then decide if either needs a new root or can be expressed through existing analytic syntax. Scope and shared-subject constraints matter more than simply keeping roots short. Retain `stud` until its distinctive methodical activity semantics have been tested against `tent adsap` (which itself needs nested-head grammar if used literally).
