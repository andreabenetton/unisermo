# Participles and resultatives

> **Status:** normative. Confirmed 2026-08-09.

## 1. Productive suffix `-it`

GlottoDelta forms the ordinary participial/resultative stem with invariant
**`-it`**:

```text
verb root + -it -> participial/resultative form
```

The suffix is phonologically /it/ and is added directly to the bare verbal
root. It is productive and does not require consonant-specific allomorphy.

| Verb root | Verb meaning | Resultative/participle | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dik` | dire | `dikit` | detto |
| `mov` | muovere | `movit` | mosso |
| `vid` | vedere | `vidit` | visto |
| `fak` | fare / creare | `fakit` | fatto / creato |

The inserted vowel avoids difficult forms such as `*dikt`, `*movt`, `*vidt`
or `*fakt` as the regular productive paradigm.

## 2. Category and use

`-it` creates a non-finite resultative/participial form. It can describe a
referent as standing in the resulting state of the verb and supplies the
regular lexical form corresponding to Italian past-participle meanings such
as *detto*, *mosso*, *visto*, and *fatto*.

The exact syntax of compound tenses and passive constructions is a separate
question. Confirming `-it` here does **not** by itself require a particular
auxiliary or passive construction.

## 3. `-it` is no longer an abstract-noun suffix

Legacy material assigned `-it` to abstract qualities/states. That assignment
is retired. In current GlottoDelta, productive `-it` is reserved for the
participial/resultative function above.

Existing legacy examples of abstract `-it` must therefore be migrated when the
abstract-derivation system is finalized.

## 4. Productive abstract-quality suffix `-itat`

GlottoDelta uses invariant **`-itat`** to derive abstract qualities, states, and
properties from suitable lexical bases. The form continues the Latin
`-itas, -itatis` family and its Romance descendants while remaining clearly
distinct from participial/resultative `-it`.

Canonical examples:

- `bon` -> `bonitat` = bonta / goodness
- `klar` -> `klaritat` = chiarezza / clarity
- `uman` -> `umanitat` = umanita / humanity

The division of labor is therefore fixed: **`-it` = participial/resultative**,
while **`-itat` = abstract quality/state/property**. This also makes forms such
as `umanitat` fully regular rather than lexical exceptions.
