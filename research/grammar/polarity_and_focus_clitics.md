# Polarity and focus clitics

> **Status:** confirmed design decision. This specification is canonical for
> affirmative/negative responses, sentential negation, and the additive and
> negative-additive focus enclitics `-si` / `-no`.

## 1. Free polarity forms: `si` and `no`

GlottoDelta uses two short invariant free forms for polar responses:

- `si` — **YES**, affirmative response
- `no` — **NO**, negative response

`no` also serves as the invariant sentential negation particle **NOT** when
placed immediately before the verb. The free response and the pre-verbal
negator are one grammatical form with related negative polarity.

Examples:

- `si.` — yes.
- `no.` — no.
- `i no vidi` — I do not see.

The polar question marker `ka` remains separate from the answer words; `ka`
marks a question, while `si` and `no` answer it.

## 2. Additive focus enclitic: `-si`

`-si` is the invariant additive focus enclitic **ALSO / TOO / ALSO X / X TOO**,
corresponding to Italian *anche / pure* in its ordinary additive use.

The enclitic is written with a hyphen and attaches to the right edge of the
constituent that receives additive focus.

Examples:

- `i-si vidi` — I too see / I also see.
- `u-si vidi` — you too see / you also see.
- `omo-si vida` — the man too sees / the man also sees.
- `tik om-si vida` — this person too sees / this person also sees.
- `omni om-si ...` — all people too / all the people also ...

The free word `si` and the enclitic `-si` are distinguished by syntax and
orthography: free `si` is an affirmative polarity word; bound `-si` marks
additive focus on a constituent.

## 3. Negative-additive focus enclitic: `-no`

`-no` is the invariant negative-additive focus enclitic **NEITHER / NOT EVEN /
NOT ... EITHER**, corresponding to Italian *neanche / nemmeno* when it marks a
focused constituent.

Like `-si`, it is written with a hyphen and attaches to the right edge of the
focused constituent.

Examples:

- `i-no vidi` — not even I see / neither do I see.
- `u-no vidi` — not even you see / neither do you see.
- `omo-no vida` — not even the man sees.
- `dom-no ...` — not even the house ...
- `tik om-no vida` — not even this person sees.

## 4. `-no` carries clause negation

When `-no` supplies the negative-additive meaning, it already licenses the
negative polarity of the clause. A second pre-verbal `no` is therefore not
required for the ordinary *neanche / nemmeno* reading.

Preferred:

- `i-no vidi` — not even I see.
- `dom-no ...` — not even the house ...

Not the ordinary expression of the same meaning:

- `i-no no vidi`

Any future use of multiple negative markers for special emphasis or concord
must be defined separately; it is not part of the present confirmed rule.

## 5. Symmetry

The system deliberately reuses the same polarity forms as free words and as
focus enclitics:

| Polarity | Free form | Bound focus form |
|---|---|---|
| positive | `si` — YES | `-si` — ALSO / TOO |
| negative | `no` — NO / NOT | `-no` — NEITHER / NOT EVEN |

This is grammatical reuse, not lexical ambiguity: the bound forms are always
hyphenated and attached to the focused constituent, while the free forms stand
alone or occupy their independent syntactic position.

## 6. Scope

The enclitic scopes over the constituent to which it attaches. For a multiword
nominal constituent it is written on the final word of that constituent:

- `tik om-si` — this person too
- `tik om-no` — not even this person
- `tik omni om-si` — all these people too
- `tik omni om-no` — not even all these people

This right-edge rule keeps the focus domain visible without requiring agreement
or inflection on the other words of the noun phrase.
