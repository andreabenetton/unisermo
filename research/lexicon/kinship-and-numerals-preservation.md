# Prior kinship morphology and numeral decisions — preservation note

This note prevents the 100-concept expansion from replacing already discussed family-relation morphology with unrelated Latin-looking roots.

## Earlier kinship design (not yet independently ratified in the confirmed lexicon)

A **neutral relational stem** plus `-o` for male and `-e` for female was preferred in prior discussion. These are historical **candidate analyses**, not established canonical entries:

| Relation | Neutral | Male | Female |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parent | `par` | `paro` father | `pare` mother |
| Offspring | `fil` | `filo` son | `file` daughter |
| Grandparent | `av` | `avo` grandfather | `ave` grandmother |
| Sibling (alternative options) | `germ` or `sib` | `germo` / `sibo` | `germe` / `sibe` |
| Uncle/aunt | `avun` | `avuno` | `avune` |
| Cousin | `kusin` | `kusino` | `kusine` |
| Spouse | `spus` | `spuso` | `spuse` |
| Niece/nephew in uncle/aunt relation | `nep` | `nepo` | `nepe` |

The confirmed lexicon currently contains **`frat` = brother**. The previous neutral-stem sibling proposal does not supersede it without an accepted decision.

Distinguish `child` (young person) from `offspring/descendant` and `family` (collective household/kin group) from a particular relative. `mother=mater`, `father=pater`, `child=infan` are **withdrawn as standalone expansion proposals**, not replaced by newly canonized `pare`, `paro`, `fil` or `infan`.

## Existing canonical numerals and relational iteration

`language/numerals.md` defines:

- 0–9: **`nul`, `uno`, `bis`, `ter`, `for`, `kin`, `ses`, `set`, `oto`, `nin`**.
- 10, 100, 1000: **`dek`, `sen`, `mil`**.
- Numerals concatenate into **one word** (e.g. `dekbis` 12, `bisdekkin` 25, `tersenbisdekuno` 321). The historic `duo` / `cin` spellings and separated numeral notation are not current.
- The canonical `jer` is an iteration/step operator, including genealogical relation steps: `bisjer` = two iterations/steps. In a genealogy, `bisjerROOT` means applying an identified relation twice, not a new base numeral and not an automatically accepted specific relative term.

**Do not invent separate numeral vocabulary or kinship-degree numerals** in future batches. Before formalizing genealogical compounds, reconcile `jer` with the exact relational stem, compose the form under accepted phonology, and verify it against existing kinship decisions. All proposed family terms still require collision, semantic and morphological review.
